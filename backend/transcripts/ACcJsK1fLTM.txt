Working with precious metals is fascinating, especially now with how far casting technology has advanced. Today I'm using real sterling silver to make a miniature model of our off-road wheelchair. It's a little gift for the guys who have been working at our wheelchair factory for the past five years. Half a decade is a long time, and I know that if you take care of the team, the team will take care of the rest.

We start creating the shape of our silver with a rather large wax 3D printer. This wax printer prints two types of wax at the same time. The white low melting point wax acts as a dissolvable support for the upper layers and, of course, the red structural wax has a higher melting point. You'll see how it works in a second. You can tell how detailed a wax printer can get with the antlers on this moose. We use warm isopropyl alcohol to dissolve the white wax, leaving behind just the red structure, which, of course, has our sprues attached to the bottom.

The sprues act as a funnel or a channel for the silver to flow through when we go to cast the silver. But first, we have to make a plaster cast of the wax. This plaster fills all internal crevices of the wax. We do this because the red wax has a vaporizing point of about 900° Fahrenheit. So, when we go to pull it out of the oven after it's been cooking for a few hours, all of the wax will be gone, vaporized, leaving behind a perfect replica of our rig in the void of the plaster. This is why it's called lost wax casting, because the wax is now lost.

Then we take our plaster investment and put it into a tabletop casting machine. This single machine is able to take our bag of sterling silver shot and heat up the little granules to a melting point of 1000 degrees, which looks pretty amazing. Thumbs up for that. At the same time, the machine is pulling a vacuum on the chamber to reduce the oxidation of the metal. There we go.

Once the liquid silver and the plaster cast are both to temperature, the chamber is then rotated, which allows the silver to flow into the void, perfectly replicating the shape of our 3D printed wax sculpture. To reveal the sculpture, we then quench the blazing hot precious metal in a bowl of water and break away the plaster. It's cool how the silver flowed through those sprues into the mold. The exterior of the silver casting is slightly burned on the outer layer thanks to the high temperature of the casting process, but a quick dip in an ultrasonic bath brings back the silvery gleam.

To polish up our masterpiece, it starts off in a vibratory tumbler for a full day. Then we'll cut off the excess silver sprues and polish up the rest. Meanwhile, we're carving a mountain out of a block of wood. This is the same wood that we make our wooden wheelchair push rims out of. Then we can laser engrave the foundation, and there we have it. A perfect replica of our off-road wheelchair cast in solid silver.
