When Bambu Lab heard that we were making accessibility more affordable at my wheelchair factory, they were kind enough to send us over some of the best 3D printers on the market. This one's called the Bamboo H2S, and we've already put them to good use prototyping and building wheelchairs. But today, we're going to put them to work 3D printing a toddler's accessibility device.

This wheelchair was designed by Make Good, a nonprofit company whose mission is also to make accessibility more affordable, in this case, free. There are good people out there in the world. We're able to build it through the power of inclusive design and 3D printing. So, when someone who's in need of a toddler mobility trainer, submits a request through the Make Good website, we signed up and started printing the parts. It's a rather complex print, taking 24 different print cycles, totaling for about 200 hours of print time. And literally every part of this chair is 3D printed, except, of course, the metal casters, some bolts, and the wheel bearings.

With all the parts hot off the printers, time for assembly. Most of the 3D printed connect together in interlocking joints, like a little Lego. These are then reinforced with a 3D printed pin that locks it into place. The two connecting pieces also have these dots that line up, so you can tell when they're fully connected. The caster wheel gets pressed in between two of the pieces and another pin locks it all into place, which makes our new kids wheelchair super sturdy. The bolts I mentioned earlier go into the foot plate to make it durable and adjustable. Two smaller casters get locked into place with the same pin system, and then we assemble the large wheels for the 3D printed tire. We use a rubbery filament this time around that allows the tire to flex and bend just like a normal tire. Four more pins lock it together, and then we add two bearings to each wheel that allow it to spin with minimal friction. All that's left is to add these foamy 3D printed cushions and the 3D printed straps, and this thing is ready to go. Our Bambu Lab H2S personal manufacturing hub handled the build perfectly.

And we're super excited to be part of this project and to help make accessibility more accessible.
